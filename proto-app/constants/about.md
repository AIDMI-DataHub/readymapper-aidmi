ReadyMapper is a digital tool that puts important disaster-related information in one place, making it easier and faster for emergency response teams to coordinate and deliver aid where it's needed most.

## Functionalities & Uses

### Select Disaster Type

- Choose the disaster type and specific event from the left pane

### Select Geographical Areas of Interest

- Once the event data loads, navigate around the zoomed in Area of Interest to understand the extent of the disaster impact
- Access pre-loaded data at the ACS place-level for US specific disasters and GADM Admin 2 level for international disasters.
- Switch to ACS County or GADM Admin 1 for aggregate statistics using the bottom left panel
- Press play on the time-slider on top of the screen to see how the disaster progresses over time.

### View Community Vulnerability Data

- Gain insights into community vulnerability through metrics like elderly population percentage and poverty levels.
- View detailed information on power outages, both overall and at the place level. Power outages are primarily available for US specific disasters
- A table will display changes in power outages as the disaster progresses.

### Analyze Population Movement

- Use the map to see where people are moving during a disaster.
- Red dots show decreasing populations; blue dots indicate increasing populations.
- Yellow dots represent areas with below-average population movement.
- Press play to see how population densities change over time.
- This helps identify where to allocate resources and deliver aid.

### Local Population Movement Analysis: Utilizing Side Panel Controls

- Select your desired location and timeframe and then analyze directional population flows with the 'To/From' toggle feature available in the side panel.
- Observe percentage change in population density on the table

### Assess Infrastructure and Healthcare Capacities

- View locations of healthcare facilities in the chosen area.
- Different symbols and colors indicate facility types and capacities.
- Click on a place or county to visualize an overlay showing driving distances to healthcare facilities. Currently available for US specific disasters

### Create and Share a Custom Report

- Select areas of interest and generate tailored reports using the "Generate Report" feature
- Customize the report's content and order based on your needs.
- Add a summary at the top for an overview of key findings.
- Save, share, or print the report as needed.

### Access Historical Data

- View past disaster data to identify patterns and improve future preparedness.

## About Us

[CrisisReady](https://www.crisisready.io/) is a research platform at the [Harvard Data Science Initiative](https://datascience.harvard.edu/) and [Direct Relief](https://www.directrelief.org/) that advances data-driven decision making in emergency response. As a multifunctional response organization, CrisisReady provides solutions that bridge the technical, regulatory, and translational gaps that hinder the integration of novel data into the field. The platform features data products, repositories, and tools developed with interdisciplinary research teams worldwide.

Our team develops scalable expertise in Data Readiness, Methods Readiness, and Translational Readiness — three concepts that extend throughout CrisisReady’s body of work — by providing actionable analyses that help policymakers and agencies make decisions on the deployment of material aid and personnel during emergency events. 

CrisisReady’s network of collaborators, which consists of world-class researchers at Harvard University, technology companies, government agencies, and the global Community of Practice, ensures that stakeholders inform our collective mission to support equitable field response around the world.

The team at CrisisReady identifies and analyzes large-scale data required to respond to disasters so that timely access can be pre-negotiated among data brokers and response agencies. Through these efforts, the platform ensures that access is appropriately negotiated between the companies who claim ownership of data, the communities from which the data are generated, and the researchers and policymakers who use the data.
